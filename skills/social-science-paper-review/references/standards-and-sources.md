# Standards and Sources

Use these sources only when they fit the paper type. Do not cite them as decoration; use them to calibrate what counts as adequate reporting and review evidence.

## General Peer Review

- Taylor & Francis reviewer guidance: use for review structure, constructive tone, and separating major from minor issues.
- Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE): use when confidentiality, reviewer ethics, duplicate publication, data fabrication, plagiarism, or conflicts of interest appear relevant.

## Reporting Standards

- APA Journal Article Reporting Standards (JARS): use for quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods reporting expectations in psychology and adjacent social sciences.
- EQUATOR Network: use as a gateway for reporting guidelines when the design maps to established checklists.
- STROBE: use for observational quantitative studies.
- CONSORT: use for randomized trials or intervention studies.
- PRISMA: use for systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
- COREQ or SRQR: use for qualitative interviews, focus groups, ethnography-adjacent qualitative reporting, or qualitative health/social research.

## Social-Science Review Calibration

Prioritize these judgment dimensions:

1. Research question importance and answerability.
2. Theoretical contribution and mechanism clarity.
3. Conceptual consistency from theory to measurement or interpretation.
4. Fit between design and claim type.
5. Methodological rationale: why the chosen method fits the question, theory, materials, and knowledge claim.
6. Evidence transparency and credibility.
7. Whether citations are explained in the author's own analytical voice rather than merely listed.
8. Handling of alternatives, counterexamples, confounders, or rival explanations.
9. Scope conditions and external validity.
10. Ethical handling of people, groups, communities, and sensitive data.
11. Writing mechanics that affect review quality: typos, redundant words, awkward sentences, and too many or too few transition markers.
